# OpenThrone — Game Design Philosophy

> "A slow, text-driven strategy game where power grows quietly, reputation spreads loudly, and every decision leaves a public scar."

## Core Pillars

These are non-negotiable. If any of these drift, it stops being *this* game.

1. **Asynchronous, turn-based** — No twitch skill. All intent.
2. **Text-first, imagination-led** — No animations, no graphical fluff.
3. **Persistent identity & reputation** — History will matter.
4. **Slow power curve** — Days, not hours. Stats go up slowly; options widen meaningfully.
5. **Player-driven conflict** — Drama emerges organically, not from scripted events.

Everything else is optional.

---

## Core Gameplay Loop

**Login → Decide → Commit → Wait → Consequences**

1. Player logs in
2. Receives a fixed number of **turns** (regenerating every 30 minutes, hard cap on stored)
3. Chooses how to allocate them: Economy, Military, or Influence
4. Actions resolve immediately or on a short delay
5. Player leaves knowing outcomes are unfolding *without them*

Waiting should feel strategic and tense — not punishing.

---

## Player Stats

Only what creates **interesting tension**:

| Stat | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Power** | Combat effectiveness (offense, defense, spy, sentry) |
| **Gold** | Economic leverage |
| **Influence** | Social and political weight (alliances, reputation) |
| **Reputation** | How others perceive you — visible, earned, scarring |

---

## Actions

### Economy
- Work / Trade
- Invest (slow, compounding)
- Raid (high risk, social consequences)

### Military
- Train troops
- Attack player
- Defend / fortify

### Influence
- Ally / Betray
- Threaten / Support
- Sabotage / Spy

**Key rule: Every action should create a narrative artifact** — a log entry, a public notice, or a private message.

---

## Combat

- Deterministic-ish — clear inputs, clear outputs
- Results shown as **battle summaries**, not dice rolls
- Combat should **change the political landscape**, not just stats
- Losses sting socially, not just numerically

Example: *"You overwhelmed X with superior numbers, but your reputation suffered for attacking a weaker opponent."*

---

## Social Layer

Minimum viable social:

- **Public activity feed** — actions are visible
- **Player profiles** — identity matters
- **Alliances** — player-organized factions
- **Public reputation markers** — titles, colors, history

No chat spam. No global memes. Social tension should emerge from gameplay, not a chatbox.

---

## Progression Without Power Creep

Progression = **unlocking decisions**, not just bigger numbers.

- New action types
- Specializations (economic tyrant vs warlord vs spymaster)
- Reputation-based perks
- Alliance-level progression

---

## Explicitly Out of Scope

Hard no's, now and forever:

- Gacha / lootboxes
- Real-time combat
- Multiple currencies beyond gold
- Full graphical avatars
- Pay-to-skip / pay-to-win

If it can't be explained in one sentence, it doesn't belong.

---

## Success Criteria

The game is working when:

- Players talk about **other players**, not mechanics
- Feuds form naturally
- Logging in feels *important*, not habitual
- Losses sting socially, not just numerically
- New players can explain the game to a friend in under 30 seconds
